## Summary

The authors created a complete connectome of the male *Drosophila* central nervous system, covering brain and nerve cord. The reconstruction contains 166,700 neurons and 11,710 neuron types, and is fully proofread and annotated, including expression of the sex-determination factors *fruitless* and *doublesex*.

A central contribution is the first comprehensive synaptic-resolution comparison between male and female fly brains. The researchers identify 8,069 isomorphic neuron types, alongside 138 dimorphic types, 289 male-specific types and 71 female-specific types. The broad sensory and motor periphery is mostly shared across sexes; differences are instead concentrated in higher-order brain regions that integrate information.

The paper analyses visual, auditory, olfactory and taste pathways, showing common organisational principles despite different sensory modalities. Within higher centres, male-specific wiring clusters into hotspots around male-specific neurons or arbors. Dimorphic neurons can reroute information between the male and female networks, offering candidate circuit mechanisms for sex-specific behavioural outputs.

## Why This Matters

The male whole-central-nervous-system connectome gives researchers a route from sensory input to motor output at synaptic resolution. Combined with existing female datasets and the fly's genetic tools, it makes it possible to formulate and test circuit-level explanations of sex-specific behaviour rather than focusing only on individual neurons.

## Caveats and Limitations

- Connectivity does not reveal synaptic strength, state-dependent activity or causal behavioural function.
- Sex differences in wiring need targeted physiological and behavioural tests.
- The dataset represents particular specimens and conditions, so population variability remains important.
- The work is a resource and hypothesis generator; it does not by itself show why each dimorphic connection exists.
- Translation to mammals should focus on general circuit principles, not direct anatomical equivalence.

## Key Takeaways

- The complete male *Drosophila* central nervous system connectome contains 166,700 neurons.
- It supports the first brain-wide synaptic-resolution comparison of male and female fly circuitry.
- Most sex-specific and dimorphic wiring is found in higher-order integrative centres.
- Sensory and motor peripheries are largely isomorphic across sexes.
- The resource enables end-to-end study of sensorimotor circuits and sex-specific behaviour.
