## Summary

The occipitotemporal cortex (OTC) helps us recognize and interpret objects. One influential account proposes that its broad, large-scale organization is shaped by two major dimensions: **animacy** (whether something is alive) and **real-world size**. This study asks whether those dimensions really stand out when compared with a wider range of object properties and more diverse images.

The researchers analysed a densely sampled fMRI dataset from **three participants** who viewed thousands of natural images spanning 720 object categories. They examined cortical response patterns in relation to **15 object properties**, including animacy, real-world size, agency, naturalness, humanness, moveability, heaviness, hardness, curvature and manipulability. The participants performed an oddball-detection task while viewing the images. To compare the properties, the authors mapped their ratings onto the fMRI responses, tested how smoothly each property varied across the cortical surface, and estimated each property’s independent contribution using hierarchical partitioning across combinations of predictors.

The study confirmed broad animacy-related patterns in OTC. Regions associated with face and body processing tended to respond more to animate categories, while scene-selective regions tended to respond more to inanimate categories. Size also showed broad organization, but with a more detailed pattern than earlier accounts suggested: preferences differed across regions, and the responses distinguished large from small animals as well as large from small objects.

The wider comparison showed that animacy and size were not unique. All 15 tested properties produced smooth, broad cortical maps. Properties correlated with animacy, such as agency and moveability, had similar topographies; properties associated with size, such as heaviness and manipulability, also resembled its map. When the researchers estimated the properties’ independent contributions, several still explained response variance in areas traditionally linked to animacy or size. Moveability contributed more than animacy in some face- and body-related regions, for example, and the ability to be moved contributed more than size in some scene-related regions. No one property consistently dominated across the cortex.

The authors conclude that OTC organization may be better understood as a multidimensional representation that supports flexible access to many object properties. Animacy and size remain relevant, but the data do not support reducing the broad functional organization of OTC to those two dimensions—or to a small set of concepts—alone.

## Why This Matters

A property can make a smooth map across cortex without being the unique principle that organizes that cortex. This study shows why researchers need to compare proposed dimensions with other correlated properties and test how much each adds independently.

The findings refine a familiar model of visual cortex: animacy and size are part of the picture, but features such as moveability, agency and heaviness may also matter. The results fit a view in which visual representations can support different ways of describing and using objects, rather than assigning each broad cortical region to one simple concept.

## Caveats and Limitations

- The analysis used three densely sampled participants. Repeated scanning provides detailed data for each person, but three people are still a small sample for establishing how consistent the maps are across individuals.
- fMRI associations do not show that a property causes the cortical pattern or that the brain explicitly computes that property as an organizing rule.
- Many object properties are correlated. Hierarchical partitioning helps estimate their separate contributions, but the estimates still depend on the chosen ratings, predictors and modelling assumptions.
- Ratings were assigned to object categories, while the images within a category can vary in pose, context, visual complexity and other image-level features.
- The modelling was largely linear. Interactions among object properties or more complex response patterns may not be fully captured.
- The conclusions apply to the dataset, task and cortical measurements studied. They do not establish a complete account of object perception or all visual-cortex organization.

## Key Takeaways

- Animacy and real-world size both relate to broad response patterns in occipitotemporal cortex.
- Size-related organization was more detailed than a simple large-object/small-object division, including distinctions among animals.
- Other properties, including moveability and agency, also produced smooth maps and explained independent response variance.
- No one of the 15 tested object properties dominated across OTC.
- The study supports a multidimensional account of visual object representation, while its small participant sample and correlational methods limit causal and population-wide conclusions.
