## Summary

This ISPOR Task Force report provides evidence-informed recommendations for using generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) across systematic literature review (SLR) tasks in health technology assessment and health economics and outcomes research. It synthesizes a rapid evidence assessment of English-language biomedical studies published from November 2022 to July 2025, then applies a structured task-level framework spanning eight evaluation domains and expert consensus.

The strongest support is for high-recall title-and-abstract screening and structured first-pass data extraction when these are embedded in human-in-the-loop workflows with explicit oversight. Evidence for full-text screening, qualitative synthesis, and report writing is more conditional and depends on workflow design, validation, and risk management. Autonomous end-to-end reviews and autonomous risk-of-bias judgments are not supported by the current evidence.

The authors recommend that teams evaluate suitability task by task, pre-specify escalation and review rules, retain human accountability at every decision point, and transparently document how GenAI was used.

## Why This Matters

This is directly useful to review teams considering AI-assisted workflows. It distinguishes augmentation from replacement: GenAI may reduce workload in selected, well-governed steps, but methodological responsibility and final decisions remain with human reviewers. Its task-specific guardrails are especially relevant where reviews inform HTA, reimbursement, or policy decisions.

## Caveats and Limitations

The recommendations reflect a fast-moving and heterogeneous evidence base through July 2025, so they will require periodic updating. The underlying studies vary in datasets, models, outcomes, and evaluation approaches, making quantitative pooling inappropriate. The report focuses on full systematic reviews in HEOR, HTA, and regulatory contexts; transfer to rapid or targeted reviews needs careful adaptation. This is a journal pre-proof rather than the final version of record.

## Key Takeaways

- Use GenAI to augment defined tasks, not to replace accountable human reviewers.
- High-recall title-and-abstract screening and structured first-pass extraction have the strongest current support.
- Full-text screening, synthesis, and writing need more conditional, validated workflows.
- Autonomous risk-of-bias assessment and end-to-end review production are not supported.
- Report GenAI use transparently and pre-specify how errors, conflicts, and escalations are handled.
