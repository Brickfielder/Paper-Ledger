## Summary
This study asked how the brain uses recent sensory history to shape current decisions. The authors worked in mice performing a visual discrimination task in which choices depended on the trial-by-trial difference between consecutive stimuli, measured as |ΔDir|. They focused on a thalamocortical pathway from lateral posterior (LP), the rodent homolog of pulvinar, to the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC). The motivation was that ACC and related prefrontal areas have been linked to attention and predictive processing, making them plausible sites for history-dependent evaluation of evidence. The main manipulation was optogenetic perturbation of LP-ACC axons during the task. Disrupting this pathway altered how animals evaluated current sensory evidence based on recent history. In particular, the manipulation produced choice biases that depended on |ΔDir|. The authors also used two-photon imaging to examine the activity of LP-ACC axons. These axons encoded stimuli in a task-dependent low-dimensional curved manifold. The geometry of that representation scaled with |ΔDir|, so larger deviations from recent evidence were emphasized more strongly. The authors interpret this as a contrastive representation that compares current input against recent sensory context. Together, the results support a circuit-level role for LP-ACC in implementing history-referenced sensory evaluation during decision-making. Because the evidence comes from both perturbation and imaging, the paper links circuit function to a specific representational code rather than relying on correlation alone.

## Why This Matters
This work helps explain how the brain turns a stream of sensory inputs into decisions that depend on what just happened a moment ago. It points to a specific thalamocortical pathway, rather than only cortex, as part of the machinery for context-sensitive perception.
## Caveats and Limitations
The evidence is based on a specific visual task in mice, so it is not yet clear how broadly the mechanism generalizes across behaviors, sensory modalities, or species. The summary available here is abstract-only, so details about effect size, controls, and how robust the manifold analysis was across animals and task conditions are limited. As with any circuit perturbation study, interpretation also depends on how selectively the optogenetic manipulation targeted LP-ACC axons and whether downstream network effects contributed to the observed biases.
## Key Takeaways
- LP-ACC is proposed as a thalamocortical pathway for history-dependent decision-making.
- Optogenetic disruption of LP-ACC axons altered choice behavior in a |ΔDir|-dependent way.
- Two-photon imaging showed stimulus coding in a low-dimensional curved manifold.
- The manifold geometry scaled with deviation from recent sensory evidence.
- The study supports a contrastive, history-referenced code for sensory evidence.
