## Summary

This paper asks whether information itself has value to a mouse, even when
learning it cannot improve the eventual reward. In an odour-guided task,
water-restricted mice could choose between a port that would reveal whether a
later water reward would occur and one that would not. The reward probability
was identical. The mice nevertheless preferred the information option and, in a
separate manipulation, were willing to accept less water to receive it. The
preference also decreased when the wait before reward was shortened, consistent
with a reduced value of advance information.

The authors then imaged populations of orbitofrontal cortex (OFC) neurons with
a miniature microscope while mice learnt the task. Odours predicting that
information would become available and odours predicting water reward recruited
largely distinct neural populations. The information-related representation
emerged through learning, scaled with the behavioural value of information, and
could be decoded separately from water value.

Population analyses, including a latent-variable embedding model, reinforced
this separation: information and water reward occupied different dimensions of
OFC activity. The result is a neural account of why an animal might work to
reduce uncertainty even when it cannot alter what will happen.

## Why This Matters

Information seeking is often treated as a sophisticated human behaviour, but
this work demonstrates a measurable form of intrinsic information value in
mice. That makes it possible to study its neural and circuit basis with
approaches unavailable in humans or primates.

The distinction between information value and reward value is especially useful.
It suggests that the desire to know is not fully reducible to a conventional
external reward signal, even though both forms of value are represented in a
decision-making region such as OFC.

## Caveats and Limitations

The study models a specific form of curiosity: choosing advance knowledge about
a probabilistic water reward. It cannot show that all information seeking, or
human intellectual curiosity, depends on the same computation.

The imaging establishes correlates of information and reward value in OFC but
does not test their causal necessity. The paper also leaves open how these OFC
representations interact with dopamine, the basal ganglia and other circuits
that may encode uncertainty, prediction error or learned value.

## Key Takeaways

- Mice prefer information that resolves uncertainty even when it cannot alter
  the reward outcome.
- They will trade some water reward to obtain that information.
- OFC neurons develop a representation of predicted information value during
  learning.
- Information value and water value are encoded by largely distinct OFC
  populations and population dimensions.
- The strength of information-value coding tracks its behavioural value.
- The task provides an experimental mouse model for the neural basis of
  curiosity-like information seeking.
